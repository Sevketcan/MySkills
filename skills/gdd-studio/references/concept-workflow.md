# Concept discovery

Use this mode to establish a game concept before decomposing it into systems.

## Conversation sequence

Do not present these as a long questionnaire. Use known context, then ask only the next decision that changes the design.

1. **Intent and constraints**
   - desired player feeling and core fantasy;
   - genre, platform, session shape, audience, business model;
   - team size, experience, time, budget, content capacity, and accessibility constraints.
2. **Core identity**
   - one-sentence elevator pitch;
   - primary player verb;
   - unique hook compared with named references;
   - what is deliberately faithful to a reference and what must differ.
3. **Loops**
   - moment-to-moment loop;
   - short objective cycle;
   - complete session;
   - long-term progression or end condition.
4. **Experience chain**
   - target feeling;
   - desired emergent behavior;
   - mechanics that cause that behavior;
   - feedback that makes cause and effect legible.
5. **Pillars and anti-pillars**
   - keep 3–5 non-negotiable pillars;
   - give every pillar a concrete design test;
   - define at least three anti-pillars to prevent predictable scope creep.
6. **Audience and motivation**
   - specify the primary audience rather than “gamers”;
   - identify whether autonomy, competence, relatedness, challenge, discovery, expression, relaxation, or social play is central;
   - state who the game is not for.
7. **Scope and validation**
   - define one core fun hypothesis;
   - list the minimum features needed to test it;
   - list features explicitly excluded from the prototype;
   - record design, technical, market, and scope risks.

## Quality checks

- A stranger can explain what the player repeatedly does after reading the pitch.
- The unique hook changes play rather than only presentation.
- Every pillar can reject at least one plausible feature.
- The prototype tests one uncertain assumption instead of demonstrating every feature.
- Reference games are decomposed into specific lessons; the concept is not described only as a mixture of titles.
