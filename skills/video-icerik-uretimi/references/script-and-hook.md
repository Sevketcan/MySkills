# Script and hook: 30-60 second short-form video

Use this for Reels, Shorts and TikTok scripts, or for the opening of a longer video. If there is a reference video or ad, break it down first with [reference-analysis.md](reference-analysis.md) and build on its structure (YT5). If earlier videos have retention data, start from where viewers dropped (see step 6 in [packaging.md](packaging.md)).

## Steps

1. **Stakes (YT1).** Set up:
   - a character the viewer sides with (the user, a customer, you);
   - something they could lose (money, time, an opportunity);
   - a deadline.

   Make all three clear in the first 2-3 seconds.
2. **Opening pattern (YT4).** Pick one of these openings to test:
   - cold open: drop the viewer into someone's moment;
   - show the object: the result in hand, then a surprise;
   - "you" view: a familiar situation;
   - bold statement: one sentence that sounds wrong;
   - personal confession;
   - raw footage.

   Don't open with "Hi, in this video…" (YT1).
3. **Loop (YT2, YT3).** stakes → big question → twist → re-hook.
   - **Big question:** don't ask it outright; leave the information incomplete. Keep to one question and delay the answer (YT2).
   - **Twist:** "everyone thinks ___, actually ___". It must not contradict what was said at the start.
   - **Re-hook:** "but that wasn't the real problem".
   - **Rhythm:** a starting point to test is 2-3 loops in 45-60 seconds, a new curiosity roughly every 15-20 seconds (YT3).
4. **Skeleton to test (YT21).** One way to lay the loop out sentence by sentence:
   1. hook: 1-2 sentences; the subject is clear in the first one;
   2. lock-in: 2-4 sentences that convince the viewer the rest is worth it. Pick one route: hint at the proof, hint at the change they'll feel, contrast with the common belief, a method not named yet, answer the objection early, or raise the hook's claim;
   3. body blocks: at least two, 3-5 sentences each, a non-obvious idea and then its support (fact, example or reason);
   4. re-hook: one sentence between blocks that opens a new question;
   5. CTA: one sentence, ideally a free resource that matches the topic. Without a CTA, close in 1-2 sentences.

   The loop maps onto it: stakes and big question → hook and lock-in; twist → a body block's unexpected idea; re-hook → re-hook.
5. **Check.**
   - Is it clear within 3 seconds who could lose what?
   - Is there one clear question in the viewer's head?
   - Is the twist consistent with the opening?
   - Is it as short as the content allows? Cut padding: on Shorts, average view duration should be close to the full length, and every extra sentence works against it (YT22).
   - Does each sentence lead into the next, and does it flow when read aloud?
   - Consider a separate pass for common LLM writing tics (YT27): denying one thing only to state the real point, announcing importance ("here's the part that matters"), closing a paragraph on an aphorism, choppy fragment rhythm, ornate metaphors, and knocking down a reading nobody proposed. Say the thing plainly instead. The pass must not add new numbers, names or quotes, and emphasis chosen on purpose (the hook, the lock-in) stays.
6. **Output (YT6).**
   - The script, with the part it serves next to each sentence.
   - Three alternative opening lines.
   - The script's weakest point.
   - Ask the user to add their own example and figures; the first draft is only a starting point. A human checks the hook and every factual claim before publishing.

## Notes

- For a channel that publishes from scripts regularly, consider keeping a separate script template or skill per channel and teaching it from recordings: compare the script with what was actually said, and write a correction back once it has recurred 3-4 times (YT23).
- If the user records the script themselves, see "Before recording" in [talking-edit.md](talking-edit.md) (YT29).
