# AI search: being cited and recommended

AI answers mostly reuse what classic search already finds. Good SEO, crawlable and indexed pages and plain answers come first. There is no special AI shortcut; `llms.txt` and markup are not one (K27).

## Steps

1. **Build the queries.** For the top 5 commercial keywords, form "best + keyword + year" (in the site's language, for example "en iyi … 2026").
2. **Collect the cited lists.**
   - Gather the list articles that appear for these queries in AI Overviews, AI Mode and ChatGPT sources.
   - Repeat for at least 7 days and keep the ones that keep appearing.
   - Record date, country, device, engine and mode (K5, K23).
3. **Measure three things separately:** the brand being mentioned, a link to the site, and the brand being recommended.
   - A run that returned no answer is missing data, not "not mentioned".
   - One answer is one observation (K23).
4. **Draft outreach** for the lists the site is missing from: short and personal. The user sends them.
   - Back every claim in an email with a primary-source URL.
   - Drop what cannot be verified.
5. **Propose a statistics or report page** built from data the site owns (prices, its own measurements, test results). It is the cleanest way to earn links and citations (K22).
6. **Suggest "best" and the year** in the titles of suitable commercial pages, through the brief in [page-workflow.md](page-workflow.md).
7. **Track AI traffic** as its own channel in the site's analytics. If available, read Bing Webmaster's AI Performance report: which pages are cited, and which queries the AI typed (K10).

## Notes

- On the page itself, state the claims an AI would quote (price, duration, feature) near the top, in plain factual sentences. Give each task one official page (pricing, policies, specs) (K6).
- AI traffic is small but tends to convert better. Most customers still come from Google, so classic SEO keeps priority.
