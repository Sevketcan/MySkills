# Economy and progression audit

## Build the flow map

For every resource identify:

- sources that create it;
- sinks that destroy it;
- pools that store it and their caps;
- converters that exchange it;
- traders that transfer ownership without changing total supply;
- reset and persistence boundaries;
- time/tick processes that change it without a direct player action.

Name the authoritative owner and unit for every resource. Distinguish value creation from value transfer.

## Feedback loops

Write each loop as a causal chain. Example: capital ↑ → position size ↑ → profit per cycle ↑ → capital ↑.

For every positive loop state:

- whether the acceleration is intentional;
- what bounds or saturates it;
- when it should become noticeable;
- whether failure can recover;
- which player policy exploits it fastest.

Negative feedback can stabilize an economy but may erase mastery if it invisibly punishes success. Make dampers legible and thematic where possible.

## Career simulation

Simulate full careers or runs, not isolated transactions. Record by player policy and scenario:

- time/ticks to each meaningful purchase or unlock;
- income and spending per minute/session;
- stock distribution at milestones;
- frequency of sessions with no visible progress;
- bankruptcy, stall, or cap-hit rate;
- share of value generated by each source and consumed by each sink;
- strategy choice frequency and final outcome distribution.

For persistent economies, include inactivity and returning-player scenarios. For finite runs, include the end condition and opportunity cost of waiting.

## Dominant strategy audit

A strategy is suspect when it has equal or greater expected reward with equal or lower risk, information requirement, attention, and opportunity cost across most relevant states.

Test at least:

- naive/first-order policy;
- conservative policy;
- aggressive policy;
- optimizing or exploit-seeking policy;
- deliberately inactive policy when waiting is possible.

Do not fix dominance by adding arbitrary friction first. Examine information, opportunity costs, counter-scenarios, caps, and resource contention.

## Progression compatibility

Place on a shared time axis:

- player capability/power;
- challenge/demand;
- income;
- mandatory and optional costs;
- content/unlock cadence.

Flag incompatible curve orders, sudden slope changes, long dry intervals, and unlock clusters. Use milestone affordability distributions rather than only an average player timeline.

## Economy acceptance criteria

Criteria must be game-specific and measurable. Examples of useful shapes:

- at least X% of modeled target players reach milestone M within Y–Z minutes;
- no tested policy owns more than X% of best outcomes across the scenario matrix;
- bankruptcy remains below X% for the naive policy after onboarding;
- the 90th-percentile unlucky outcome remains below the pity/effort ceiling;
- stock remains within designed bounds through N simulated sessions;
- a positive feedback loop reaches the intended fantasy without skipping more than X progression tiers.

Treat all numeric targets as design decisions until supported by playtest or telemetry.
