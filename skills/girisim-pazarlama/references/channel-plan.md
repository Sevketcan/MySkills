# Channel plan: which channel now, and in what order

Use this when the question is where the next customers should come from.

## Steps

1. **Measure today's channels.** For each one, record what it brings from analytics, referrers or the signup source. Mark missing data as missing.
   - If organic search clicks are falling, consider first separating which visits were lost and whether revenue changed, before moving effort elsewhere. Consider counting mentions in AI answers only by the visits and customers they bring (G23). Search Console work: `seo-ai-arama`.
   - To learn what worked in the market, consider reverse-engineering a competitor's growth: find the break point in its traffic, match the moves just before it (a launch, the start of ads, a creator video, a product cycle) and label each finding as seen or inferred. Ads that have run for more than 60 days are worth shortlisting from the platforms' ad libraries: a long run means someone keeps paying for it. The libraries show no spend or results, so treat a long-running ad as a candidate, not as proof that it works (G31). Paid data tools need the user's approval.
2. **Pick one channel for the current stage (G1, G2).** The ladder below is one ordering, with stage thresholds, that worked for a B2B SaaS product. Treat it as a starting hypothesis and adapt it to the user's product: for a consumer product the first channel may be community or search.
   - **0-100 customers:** reach people who show intent directly (outbound), or answer intent questions in communities (G7). See [intent-posts.md](intent-posts.md).
   - **Early, and the product works:** share value on Reddit and in communities (G4); SEO (`seo-ai-arama`).
   - **Trust is needed:** content plus a free template or blueprint; founder-led content.
   - **Partnership stage:** influencers, newsletter sponsorships, long-running affiliate programs (G5). Paying creators and clippers: [offer-and-ads.md](offer-and-ads.md).
   - **Paid ads last,** once unit economics hold (G3). Run [offer-and-ads.md](offer-and-ads.md) before any ad spend.
3. **Write a 4-week criterion** for the chosen channel: what result counts as "working".
4. **When it works, add the next channel on top** and keep the working one running (G1).

## Channel notes

- **Reddit and communities (G4):** cheap and strong early, but only when sharing value: an educational analysis, a changelog of real features, an answer to a question that shows intent.
- **Affiliate (G5):** long-running terms (recurring or lifetime commission, a long cookie) give leverage, and affiliates' posts also bring backlinks.
- **Word of mouth and brand demand (G23):** build demand through word of mouth and searches for the brand name, not only generic search. Track brand-name searches separately.
- **AI agents (G22):** if the product does a job an agent could call, an API or MCP server listed in the official registry and in directories is a channel of its own. See [agent-distribution.md](agent-distribution.md).
- **Launch directories and awesome-lists (G34):** treat them as a cheap backlink and indexing layer for a new product, not as the revenue channel of the stage. Judge them by live links, new referring domains and payments, not by the number of submissions. Workflow: [press-and-launch.md](press-and-launch.md), section 4.
- **X (G15, G30):** posts people read after clicking do better than posts that only get the click (G15). Consider writing to the signals in X's open-source ranking code: original posts rather than replies or reposts, early likes, something worth sharing or replying to, and no untargeted posts that make people tap "not interested". That code gives video views no weight, so consider not relying on video alone for reach. Weights change; check the current code before quoting one (G30).
